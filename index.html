What counts as a plumbing emergency

A burst pipe, a sewage backup, a water heater tank that has split open, and a leak that is still running through a ceiling are emergencies. Water is still causing damage while you wait, so the job cannot wait for a scheduled slot.

A dripping tap, a slow drain, or a toilet that runs on is not an emergency. Those can wait for a weekday visit, and booking ahead is the cheaper path.

The test is simple: is water still escaping, or is waste still backing up into the house? If yes, call now. If no, write down the symptoms and book a visit.

First steps before the plumber arrives

Step one: stop the water. Find the main water shutoff, usually near the street meter or where the line enters the house, and turn it off. Then open a tap at the lowest point to drain the lines.

Then do these four things

  1. Move rugs, electronics, and papers away from the water.
  2. Note where the water is coming from and when it started.
  3. Do not use a drain that is backing up, and do not flush a toilet that is overflowing.
  4. Take a photo of the damage for your own records.

Do not try to cut into a wet wall yourself. A rushed repair on a wet wall can hide a second leak behind it, and the wall will need to dry before it can be patched properly.

What the first visit looks like

The technician arrives in uniform, lays down drop cloths, and starts with a check of the visible problem before any cutting starts. For a leak, that means pressure gauges and listening tools. For a drain, a camera inspection that shows the real blockage.

After the check comes a written estimate. It lists parts, labour, and scope before any physical work begins, and the homeowner approves it before the repair starts. If the check finds a second problem, that is a separate estimate, not a surprise on the same bill.

The repair is done to Florida Building Code standards, then pressure tested or flood tested to confirm it holds. The work zone is cleaned before the crew leaves.
