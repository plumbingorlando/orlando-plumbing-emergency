Emergency Plumbing in Orlando: What To Do When a Pipe Bursts
A burst pipe, a sewage backup, or a water heater tank that has split open cannot wait. This page explains what counts as a plumbing emergency in Orlando, Florida, what to do in the first five minutes, and what the first visit looks like — so you are ready before it happens.

What counts as an emergency
A burst pipe, a sewage backup, a water heater tank that has split open, and a leak that is still running through a ceiling are emergencies. Water is still causing damage while you wait, so the job cannot wait for a scheduled slot. A dripping tap, a slow drain, or a toilet that runs on is not an emergency — those can wait for a weekday visit.

First steps before the plumber arrives
Stop the water at the main shutoff, usually near the street meter or where the line enters the house, then open a tap at the lowest point to drain the lines.
Move rugs, electronics, and papers away from the water.
Note where the water is coming from and when it started, and take a photo for your own records.
Do not use a drain that is backing up, and do not flush a toilet that is overflowing.
What the first visit looks like
The technician lays down drop cloths, checks the visible problem before any cutting starts, and then gives a written estimate — parts, labour, and scope — before any work begins. The repair is done to Florida Building Code standards, then pressure tested or flood tested to confirm it holds.
